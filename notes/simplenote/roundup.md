# Roundup — Parliament of the Snake

Four years of notes, fourteen threads, read against the manuscript audits. This is what you decided, what the book becomes if you enact it, what it fixes, and what it is now.

## 1. What you decided

The settled position on each thread — your own doctrine, recovered from the latest and best-integrated note in each.

- **Vetko is the fear of death.** "His entire personality is not wanting to become irrelevant — dead people don't matter" (2024-09-22). Logline underneath it, never once reversed in four years: "He's so scared of being left behind that he changes the world so he isn't" (2023-02-14). The arc: "He starts wanting to bring the past back, then he decides the only way to get over it is to get rid of it" (2024-11-15).
- **The Raven was Erica / Beck Avon.** "His real past (Doug Goode, Raven) belongs only in the prequel" (2026-01-24). The truth exists; this book never states it — only the state's ignorance keeps it hidden ("no one knows she died," 2024-11-10).
- **Main-characterness is a real, transferable, inherited power.** "This book is about a world where narrative importance is inherited, and the people who notice that inheritance are the most dangerous ones in the room" (2026-01-24). John is "the narrative carrier not the emotional centre"; Palm "a meta-custodian not a saviour."
- **John is hiding, not scared.** He hides because he holds a specific, dangerous secret — not from fear, not primarily from grief. "That's why he hides, per what she told him" (2023-10-29), made concrete by "only John and vetko know the truth" (2024-11-10). Your gut-check line: "He's in marketing and he's hiding" (2025-04-12).
- **Jones's death is the bureaucratic midpoint.** "When Jones dies — nothing personal" (2022-11-24), sharpened to "She'll do" (2024-07-29) and finalized: "Jones's death is bureaucratic, not heroic," the Watch explosion is "the load-bearing midpoint pivot," and it "must never be softened, explained, or comforted afterward" (2026-01-24). Whispered, not stated (2025-06-22).
- **Structure: "ALL JONES, THEN SHE DIES THEN ALL JOHN"** (2024-04-29). Chronological handover, no flash-forward. Jones owns the first half, her death is the hinge, John owns the second.
- **Julie's fate is singular: captured, not killed.** "Julie fought back, they removed her from the estate and now they need another victim" (2025-04-12); "jones sees her when she is captured," held unconscious in a van (2025-04-14). Governed by the craft rule "don't say what happened til john sees the video, leave some mystery" (2023-04-15).
- **IT is the Pulpos brain in a tank — and John frees it.** Locked on "The thinktank is an eldritch brain in a jar" / "Bow to IT — it's both an eldritch horror and the IT department" (2023-05-05/06). The climax mechanism: "John set IT free" (2025-11-10).
- **Baker wants transcendence, and is used the whole time.** "There exists a perfect sound... I have heard it, and I must share it" (2025-11-14); "Baker takes instructions from IT all the way through" (2025-11-07).
- **Music carries the John/Reza friendship.** "Music has to be the thing" (2026-03-28) — not banter, not slang, not shared danger.
- **The dystopia is deliberately shit at its job.** "Harmony/the brain-in-a-jar is administration not godhood" (2026-01-24); "they're petrol and rusty and fucked" (2023-07-18). The world loops the year 2000 because "we fractured after no more mono culture" (2026-07-07) — the last thing everyone still shares.
- **The meta layer stays deniable.** "John does not change the world. The world keeps changing around him" (2026-01-24). Villain grandiosity on first read, literal truth on re-read; the "concept" monologues stay in the prequel.
- **The ending is a fragile peace, not an overthrow.** "The end should be a fragile peace not the overthrow of the government" (2026-06-18). Underwritten by "sadness is harder, and longer lasting, and it doesn't burn out" (2025-06-06). Drones down and Harmony gone don't equal resolution; Block escapes.
- **The secondary cast is set:** Cesira wants desperately to matter and is refused it (thrown off the roof, 610); Roger simplified to a "party man" who goes quietly; the Ethel/Esme plant-and-payoff protected as the model to reuse.

## 2. How the story changes if you enact these

Walk the book with the decisions applied. What's different from the current manuscript is marked.

**The opening reads as a claim, not just a joke.** Same cold open — Session 84 convening as mock ritual — but now the incompetence is *declared* in Act One instead of only stated twice in the back half (303, 508). The regime is petty-and-broken on purpose ("close the roads so everyone's a bit annoyed"), and Vetko's chapter epigraphs ("I have a strong belief in the transience of human nature. They'll believe whatever you tell them") make his psychology legible from page one without spending the book-three secret. **Different:** the reader now knows the shabbiness is designed, so Act One incompetence can't read as the author being sloppy.

**Jones owns the first half, and Julie's disappearance is clean and singular.** Jones searches; the not-knowing is the engine that will later pull John in. **Different:** Julie is no longer the reckless woman with a baseball bat who climbs the hill and comes home — she's a lifelong mast-protester, "politically disgruntled," *taken* because she "fought back" against the entity and "they need another victim." Her fate stops being three incompatible things (murdered by Roger / found dead in crow propaganda / mind blown) and becomes one: captured, unconscious in a van, glimpsed by Jones at the moment of her own capture — with the owed payoff being the *video* John eventually sees, the signpost the book currently never delivers.

**John reads as a man staying hidden on purpose.** Extend snippet 95 — "Eight years of nothing happening. People assume nothing happens by itself. Nothing takes work" — and his passivity flips from grief-cocoon to maintained camouflage. Showing his pass to a dead camera becomes fieldcraft (fear of being *recorded*, not hurt). **Different:** at least one beat where John suspects Palm knows who he is — which converts the whole Palm thread "from absurdist harassment into dread," because now John (and the reader) senses the state is fixated on him *for a reason*: he's one of only two people alive who know the Raven didn't die a martyr. The first Reza meeting still lands its comedy (John "reduced to shitting self"), but it's paired with a tradecraft tell so the coward read isn't the only read.

**Vetko's motive goes on the page.** The frozen-nineties freeze is revealed as terror of time, not aesthetic nostalgia. Somewhere in the Control Node rant he names what the control is actually *for*; the crack in his voice after "taking her name in vain" is shown as grief for Beck. **Different:** "Because you don't matter Ian. Nobody matters but me" stops being a stray menace line and becomes the payoff of a stated thesis — *dead people don't matter, and he refuses to be one.*

**The midpoint is a hard, cold handover.** Jones dies in the Watch/Cloudtop bombing because she is *sufficient*, not chosen — "She'll do. Nothing personal." **Different:** the death is whispered, not stated (she looks at Roger; the big moment is under-said), with no quip after the peak — the single most contested line in every reader's report, resolved toward *quieter*, not punchier. It is emphatically **not** the mythologized 2025-12-07 "soul-consuming / Jothas hallucination" version; the doctrine forbids softening or explaining it. Palm hands John the codes as she dies ("YOUR POCKET MR BLARD"), and agency transfers totally, mid-sentence, into "PART TWO." The withheld second half of her capture pays off later as the recovered Drone tape (411–418), the book's unanimous strongest dread.

**The climax is the brain-in-a-tank, and John is the one who frees it.** The ThinkTank is finally shown as what it always was in the notes — an eldritch brain in a jar, physically moved into the building ("since we moved it the results are off the charts"). **Different:** the eldritch/H'gok material becomes one legible mechanism instead of an arbitrary genre-swerve — Baker's ceiling-crawl, the screen flickering through faces (Baker, a flash of raven hair, Jones with her lips pulled back inhumanly), and Vetko's own impossibilities are all downstream of the same administration. Baker, despite having power over IT, fights it and enters the codes that free it anyway, then tries to blow it up as a last resort. "Terminate Connection Permanently? John pressed enter. The omnipresent hum stopped." **John set IT free** — culpability, not accident. And into that dead silence, for the only time in the book, Reza's illegal banger clicks on: the music-vs-Harmony fight made audible exactly once.

**Music is the friendship, made reciprocal.** A recurring song threads their scenes (The Passenger, 224 → 496). **Different:** the four emotional peaks that are currently music-*silent* get scored — Reza hums something bowed in the bunk-bed dark, so the concert-violinist-dad reveal (525–530) means "his tapes as everything he's got left of the man" rather than only explaining his accent; a hummed callback lands at the hand-holding (613–614); and "You can pick the music" becomes the book's last friendship line (623).

**The ending refuses a victory.** Cesira is thrown from the roof on Vetko's undramatic "You were never that important" / "That's beautiful — but what are you going to do with it?" — the gut-punch that subverts the final-showdown cliché, and one of the few beats where a late note and the finished page already agree exactly. **Different:** drones down and Harmony gone do *not* equal resolution. Block escapes by helicopter. The book ends small and personal — a fragile peace, not the overthrow of the government — closing on the pub (623) and the skull-mountain dream, with the quiet-sadness register the reader panel confirms is already where the book's best beats live.

## 4. The through-line

Across four years, *Parliament of the Snake* became a book about a broken country that chose to stop time because a monoculture ended and the frozen nineties were the last thing anyone still shared — and, underneath the farce, a book about one frightened old man who cannot bear to be left behind by a world that will outlive him, and so freezes it. Everything else is a branch of that single fear: the incompetent regime is administration not godhood; the brain in the tank is the machinery of control, not a god; the "main character" is a power that is inherited and noticed, never earned; John hides not because he's a coward but because he knows the one truth that would unmake the myth; Jones dies not heroically but because she'll do; and nothing gets overthrown, because the point was never victory — it was that sadness lasts longer than anger, the old has to burn before the new can start, and the world keeps changing around a man who never changes it. The notes converged, late and decisively, on a book that ends not with a won war but with a fragile peace and a pub — and means it.
