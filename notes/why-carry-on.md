# Why Carry On

*Read this when you panic. Not sentiment — findings. Every line below came out of an actual read of your actual book.*

---

## The book is good — the evidence, not a pep talk

1. **The voice is the real thing.** A jaded agency reader, primed to reject, given your *unedited* opening, said: request the full manuscript — "genuinely distinctive and commercial." Voice can't be taught or bolted on later. You have it already.
2. **The gut-punch works.** Three readers who agree on nothing all picked the same beats — Jones's death, Erica's, the drone tape. You pull off the hardest move in comedy — funny into devastating — and it lands. Most comic novels never manage it.
3. **The architecture is real, not faked.** Verified against four years of your own notes: the ending, the brain-in-a-jar climax, Vetko-as-fear-of-death, Raven-was-Erica, Jones's bureaucratic death — all backed by consistent notes, not stitched together. The Pulpos origin ties the masts, Harmony, the brain and Baker into one root. That's rare structural integrity.
4. **The villain is a three-book engine in one grave.** Vetko as mortality-terror, the meta humming underneath, a whole submerged cosmology. Genuinely great antagonist material.
5. **You're past the two stages that kill books.** 130,000 words *exist*, and every fork is now *decided*. Finishing a draft, and knowing what the thing is — that's where most books die. You're through both.

---

## What's left — and it's finishing work, not rebuilding

1. **Cut the joke that lands on the feeling.** ~28 cuts. A gag *before* a feeling works; *on top* of it, it smothers it. Pure removal.
2. **The front half is slow.** ~80 passages before the engine turns over. The scene merges fix most of it.
3. **Tidy the continuity.** The genuine slips — Delta Isol, the Montego that survives *and* explodes, the freezing July. Mindless, satisfying.
4. **Keep the depth submerged.** The one real risk of your ambition: over-explaining the machinery. When a scene wants to explain the octopus-brain-frequency stuff — don't. Imply, joke past, leave it weird.
5. **Close the execution gap.** The ideas converged; the page hasn't caught up. The brain-in-tank climax, splitting Jemima, the merges — still to actually do. That's the whole job now.

---

**The whole thing in one line:** the hard, unteachable parts are done and good. What's left is finishing work you've got a plain checklist for.

You wrote a whole novel, found out it's good, and decided every open question about it — mostly in bed, on your phone, because you *wanted* to. That's not someone who can't write a book. That's someone writing one. Ninety minutes at nine. Cross off a box. The book is finished when the boxes are.
