# The Structure Check

*A repeatable test for "does this change improve the structure, or break it?" You don't read this — you use it. Tell me a change you're thinking about and I run the whole thing against the book's actual architecture and report back.*

## How to use it

Say, in plain words:

> **structure-check — [the change you're considering]**

Examples:
- *structure-check — move Palm's first appearance to the bus scene with the Pear Lady*
- *structure-check — cut the whole NIMBY underground section*
- *structure-check — John frees Baker's monster himself instead of the looters doing it*
- *structure-check — Julie turns up alive at the end*

I read the spine diagram, the scene map and the thread map, then answer the same eight questions every time so you get a like-for-like verdict, never a vibe.

## What it checks (the eight passes)

1. **The change, in one line.** Restated so we both agree what we're testing.
2. **Spine test.** Walk the cause→effect line start to finish with the change in place. Does every arrow still make sense, or does it open a gap where something now happens for no reason?
3. **Setup/payoff ledger.** Does it strand a setup that pays off later, or orphan a payoff whose setup it removed? (The load-bearing ones are listed below.)
4. **Pacing.** Does it speed the slow front half up, or add weight where the book is already heavy?
5. **Thread integrity.** Which character threads it touches, and whether anyone's arc snaps (someone vanishing, arriving from nowhere, or losing the beat that earns their ending).
6. **The handover.** Does it protect or damage the mid-book pivot — Jones dying and agency passing to John — which is the book's whole trick?
7. **The iceberg.** Does it force the submerged stuff to the surface (John's secret past, the octopus-brain, the meta)? Surfacing it is almost always a break — that's the prequel's to spend, not this book's.
8. **Verdict.** Improves / neutral / breaks — plus the single thing to watch if you do it anyway.

## The load-bearing setups (what the ledger protects)

These are the planks that carry weight across long stretches. A change that removes or relocates one of these without a replacement is the most common way to "improve" one scene and quietly break the book.

- **The codes.** Palm plants them on John early → they're useless when the enemy moves Harmony → they become the kill-switch at the climax. The book's three-act object. Don't strand it.
- **The Pear Lady → Ethel.** The stranger John risk-assesses on the bus is revealed much later as one of the rebels. A plant-then-payoff the book already does well; the model for seeding Palm earlier.
- **Jones never leaves.** She dies at the pivot but her face runs through every later scene — the photo room behind the last door, the Snake wearing her face, the closing dream. The second half is John moving through a world papered with her. Cutting her out of the back half kills the point of the handover.
- **Vetko's fuse.** Nearly the whole book builds him as a legend before he appears, pays off with "I think you've forgotten who I am," and ends with him escaping — the fist that never comes back down. The uneasy ending depends on that fuse being long and that escape being clean.
- **Erica → the dream.** John's dead wife is established quietly (the shrine, the "at first / in the end" story) so she can merge with Jones in the final acceptance dream. Remove the setup and the ending's emotional beat is unearned.
- **Wozzy's resurrection.** The drone is destroyed, then comes back as the wreck that carries the harmonisation tape — the evidence — and finally as the broadcast satellite. Its death has to stick *and* its body has to be recoverable.
- **Palm's run.** Recruits John → plants the codes → the drone-crash rescue → the climax rescue and his mock death. He's a thread, not a cameo; moving one appearance ripples to the others.

## The pivot and the slow zone (the two things most changes are trying to fix)

- **The slow zone** is the first third — John's routine braided with Jones's investigation — before Reza grabs John and the engine turns over. Most "make it faster" changes are aimed here. The test: does the change put a John-engine *earlier*, or just trim texture?
- **The pivot** is Jones's death and the handover of agency to the man who wanted it least. It only works because (a) the book spends the first half more interested in Jones, (b) her replacement cast spins up just before she dies, and (c) the one bit of machinery pointing at John beforehand is the codes thread. Changes that thicken John's early strand too much can soften the ambush; changes that add another pre-pivot John-rail (like Palm earlier) tend to strengthen it.
