# Query Letter — draft

*A mock agent query for Parliament of the Snake — written early, just to see. Tune the bracketed bits, comps, and word count when Pass 6 comes round.*

---

Dear [Agent name],

I'm seeking representation for **PARLIAMENT OF THE SNAKE**, a work of absurdist dystopian comedy, complete at approximately 120,000 words. It's *Perfidious Albion* by way of Jasper Fforde — a state-of-the-nation satire with a body count and a laugh in the same breath — and I'm querying you because [one specific, genuine reason: a book on their list, a wishlist item, an interview quote].

Great Britain has voluntarily frozen itself in the year 2000. No new music, no new ideas, the borders sealed and the airwaves tuned to a soothing government frequency called Harmony that keeps everyone gently, chemically content. It is policed less by soldiers than by Neighbourhood Watch, and the most dangerous thing a citizen can do is get *noticed*.

Esmerelda Jones has never been good at not getting noticed. When her neighbour Julie vanishes and every official channel insists — cheerfully, relentlessly — that nothing has happened, Jones starts asking the one question the system is built to smother: *where did she go?* Her friend John Blard wants no part of it. John wants to be left completely alone, for reasons he has spent eight years and considerable effort keeping to himself.

Then, at the halfway point, the book does something it has spent two hundred pages daring you to trust it not to do: it takes Jones away. And the story — having taught you exactly who to love — hands itself to the man who wanted it least. John Blard, hiding in plain sight, is swept toward the centre of a rebellion he never chose, against a regime whose absent tyrant is running from something far larger than a country, and a machine in the Ministry basement that is not what anyone believes it is.

It's a comedy about grief, surveillance, and the horror of being seen — a book that changes what it's about at the exact moment you've stopped bracing for it.

For readers of Sam Byers, Jasper Fforde, and Nick Harkaway; for anyone who watches Black Mirror wishing it were funnier. [One or two lines of bio — anything true: prior writing, relevant background, or simply that this is your debut.]

Thank you for your time and consideration. The full manuscript is available on request.

Warm regards,
Aaron Cushion

## How to use this when the time comes
- **The `[…]` bits are yours.** The personalised "why you" line matters more than anything else in the letter — never send a query without it. The bio can be a single true sentence; debut writers query all the time.
- **Tune the word count.** If the merges/cuts bring it under 110k, say so — agents notice length, and a tighter number reads more professional for a debut.
- **Swap comps per agent.** Match their actual list. Keep two, three at most. "Black Mirror with jokes" is a good verbal logline but works better as flavour than as a formal comp.
- **Keep the iceberg submerged.** Note what this letter leaves OUT — the octopus-brain, the meta, the mantle. A query sells the hook and the voice, not the lore. Same discipline as the book itself.
